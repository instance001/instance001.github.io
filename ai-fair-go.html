Australian AI Fair-Go

Fit the tool to the job.

Australian AI policy should protect people from harmful deployments without taking away lawful user choice, local control, model plurality, accessibility support, and grassroots technical participation.

Open the evidence repo Read the principles View the evidence record

Operating principle

Give each system only what its job requires.

Fit the tool to the job. Give it only what that job requires. Keep the human in charge.

A school learning assistant, a local disability-support workflow, a small-business tool, a research rig, and a frontier cloud system do not need the same model, host, permissions, connectivity or regulatory treatment.

Policy wedge

Do not concentrate permission in order to control concentration.

Do not solve concentrated technological power by concentrating technological permission.

Rules designed to restrain powerful platforms can accidentally make those same platforms the only practical route to lawful AI participation. Australia should regulate harmful conduct, high-impact deployment and concentrated power without forcing every problem into one approved provider or architecture.

Three sovereignties

National capacity has more than one layer.

Hosting sovereignty

Domestic infrastructure, compute, data-centre and cloud capacity. This matters, but it is not the whole question.

Institutional sovereignty

Australian agencies, universities, recognised companies and standards bodies can research, procure, deploy and govern AI systems.

Broad national capability

Australians across the country can learn, inspect, run, adapt, evaluate and build useful AI systems without first passing through an institutional gate.
