Google Play releases

FMI apps for broader reach, under the same non-extractive stance.

Fractal Media Infrastructure publishes Google Play releases under the same broader public-interest model found across the instance001 ecosystem: free access, user respect, local-first design where applicable, and no dependence on surveillance-style business models.

What FMI's app release model means

FMI uses Google Play to broaden platform reach without abandoning the principles behind the open GitHub work. The aim is not to turn users into a product, but to put useful software in front of more people in forms they can actually install and use.

That means FMI releases are intended to avoid ads, in-app purchases, paywalls, and data-harvesting business models. Where the app design allows, the preference is local-first operation and user-controlled data. Licensing is stated per app, with a strong preference for copyleft and commons-preserving release terms.

Shared expectations

What you should expect from FMI app releases.

Free access

Apps are released for practical public use rather than funneling users toward extraction-first monetization patterns.

No ads

The default publishing stance is to avoid turning the software into an advertising surface.

No in-app purchases

Core access is not meant to be segmented behind purchase prompts or recurring purchase pressure.

No data-harvesting model

FMI does not treat user data extraction as the economic foundation of the product line.

Local-first where applicable

When an app can operate locally without cloud dependence, that is the preferred design direction.

Clear privacy links

Each app entry links to its own app-specific privacy page so store requirements stay tidy and legible.
