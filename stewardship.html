Stewardship and funding stance

Support is treated as continuity stewardship, not control.

Fractal Media Infrastructure approaches support, funding, and external interest through a public-interest stewardship model. The goal is to sustain open work over time without converting it into an exclusive, extractive, or hype-driven vehicle.

Core position

FMI is organized around open release, long-term public legibility, and continuity of infrastructure. Support is understood as a way to preserve and improve that work, not as a claim on ownership, editorial control, exclusive access, or private influence over priorities.

This is infrastructure, not a product roadmap. The work is released into the commons because openness is treated as a safeguard for reviewability, resilience, and correctness.

Appropriate support

Appropriate support includes grants, fellowships, institutional stipends, fiscal sponsorship, and other arm's-length mechanisms that help sustain maintenance, documentation, releases, and continued public access.

Not intended

FMI is not seeking venture framing, speculative ownership structures, exclusive licensing arrangements, private roadmap access, influencer-style patronage dynamics, or arrangements that turn public infrastructure into a gated asset.

Primary objective

Sustain and improve the public work

Support is intended to increase continuity, maintenance quality, documentation clarity, release stability, and access to the work over time.

Control

No implied governance rights

Support does not imply editorial control, special governance authority, hidden priority access, or private decision-making rights over the public corpus.

Licensing continuity

Commons-first by default

Unless a repository explicitly states otherwise, the intended default is continued release under AGPL-3.0-or-later or equivalent open terms, with third-party notices preserved where required.

Continuity uses

Maintenance, packaging, documentation, access

Reasonable uses include refactors, issue triage, testing, build reliability, glossary and atlas work, onboarding materials, benchmarks, and compute/time that improves reproducibility.

Independence and integrity

FMI is intentionally structured to avoid incentive distortion. Bottom-up attention economies, ad-hoc preferential arrangements, and visibility-driven pressure are treated as risks to epistemic integrity rather than signs of success.

The preferred outcome is simple: the work remains public, usable, forkable, and legible whether it grows, pauses, or is eventually superseded.
